How to play

  1. Setup. All 32 standard Xiangqi points are filled. Both Generals start face-up on their own back-rank centre. The other 30 pieces are face-down and shuffled together — Red and Black mixed. You never know who stands where, or whose piece it is.
  2. Turn. Each turn you step one of your pieces. There is no “flip in place” — the only way to reveal a hidden piece is to move it.
  3. Reveal by pushing. A hidden piece flips face-up the moment it finishes its move. It may turn out to be your Chariot — or your opponent’s.
  4. Hidden pieces move. A face-down piece moves according to the standard piece of the point it occupies. On a Chariot point it slides like a Chariot; on a Soldier point it steps one square forward. It flips after moving, and from then on follows its real identity.
  5. Capturing. You can capture the opponent’s face-up pieces, and hidden pieces sitting in the opponent’s half — no matter who they really belong to. Hidden pieces in your own half can never be captured; push them out to deal with them.
  6. Movement. Once face-up, standard Xiangqi rules apply: the Chariot slides, the Horse moves in an L (and its leg can be blocked), the Cannon captures over exactly one screen.
  7. House rules. Advisors and Elephants may cross the river and enter the palace. The General stays inside its own 3×3 palace. Soldiers never move backward and may step sideways once across the river.
  8. Winning. Capture the enemy General to win. A side with no legal move loses. 60 moves each with no capture and no flip is a draw.

Not the flipping game you may have played

In the classic “flip chess” (暗棋) played in Taiwan, pieces are eaten by rank — General beats Advisor, Advisor beats Elephant, and so on. That is a game of comparing sizes, with no movement to master. Flip Chess keeps the hidden board but throws away the ranking: placement is luck, the game is skill.

Features

🎴 A board you cannot read

Thirty face-down pieces shuffled into one pile. Every move is a real decision, because every move might reveal an enemy.

♟️ Real xiangqi underneath

Chariots slide, Horses can be leg-blocked, Cannons need exactly one screen. The hidden board never blunts the rules.

🤖 Play the computer

Three levels — Easy, Normal and Hard — so a first game and a hundredth game both feel right.

👥 Two players, one device

Pass the phone and play a friend. Great for a commute or a table between two chairs.

📴 Fully offline

No internet connection is ever used. Play on a plane, on the subway, anywhere.

🔒 Nothing collected

No ads, no sign-up, no account, no tracking. The app collects no data at all — read our Privacy Policy.
